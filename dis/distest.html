Welcome

Thank you for participating in our study. We value your participation and hope that the points below answer some key questions you may have.

What is the goal of our study?

The goal of our project is to develop simple and widely usable assistive technologies for individuals with color vision deficiency (CVD). Eventually, our technologies will help individuals with CVD: 1) better discriminate commonly confusing colors, and 2) communicate in unambiguous color vocabaulary with color normal individuals.

Why do you need my participation?

In order to develop our assistive technologies, we need to collect data from individuals who actually suffer from CVD. The data will be collected by asking you to perform a sequence of tasks. The data collected will help improve our internal model and assess the effectiveness of our technologies. Unlike many human labeling tasks that are meant to help train a computer, the tasks you will perform in this study are intended to help CVD individuals like yourself better navigate day to day life.

How long will this study take?

The entire study might take between 1 and 2 hours. We recognize that we are requesting a substantial time investment from you and appreciate your patience and diligence as you go through the tasks. At any time during the test you should feel free to take a break; in fact; we recommend you take breaks as needed to maintain attentiveness. During the break, you can leave the browser tab open, or you can close the browser tab or even shut down the computer. Your progress is automatically saved, so when you come back to this link, the previous state will be automatically restored.

In what environment should you take the test?

Ideally you take the study in an indoor setting where you can turn off lights and use window blinds to block outdoor lights. Make sure the lighting conditions are roughly the same before and after a break. Throughout the study the monitor should be at a distance of about 80 cm from your eyes, and you should focus your gaze at the center of the monitor. A good test is to fully extend your arm and raise your thumb; the thumb should roughly cover one color patch.

The test consists of a sequence of trials. In each trial, you will be presented with four color patches laid out as below. Your task is to identify the patch whose color differs from the other three. The placement of the differing patch is random.

You might use the Left Arrow and Right Arrow keys, which will shift all the patch colors together and might help you identify the different one. Pressing the Space resets the color shift.

Once you have identified the differing patch, indicate your choice by pressing one of the four Q/W/A/S letter keys corresponding to the patch's placement (e.g., W in the example below).

You must make a choice. Select randomly if you are unable to identify the differing patch.

After each trial, you will see random gray dots to reset your visual field, followed by the next trial.

Think of this as a game: the trials are progressively more challenging as long as you keep identifying the correct patch and less challenging if you make errors. The experiment terminates when a threshold is found.
