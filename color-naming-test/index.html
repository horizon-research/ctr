Welcome to Color Naming Test

Thank you for participating in our study. We value your participation and hope that the points below answer some key questions you may have.

What is the goal of our study?

The goal of our project is to develop simple and widely usable assistive technologies for individuals with color vision deficiency (CVD). Eventually, our technologies will help individuals with CVD: 1) better discriminate commonly confusing colors, and 2) communicate in unambiguous color vocabulary with color normal individuals.

Why do you need my participation?

In order to develop our assistive technologies, we need to collect data from individuals who actually suffer from CVD. The data will be collected by asking you to perform a sequence of tasks. The data collected will help improve our internal model and assess the effectiveness of our technologies. Unlike many human labeling tasks that are meant to help train a computer, the tasks you will perform in this study are intended to help CVD individuals like yourself better navigate day to day life.

How long will this study take?

This is a longitudinal study that will span 9 days. The activity of each day varies, which we will describe in detail later. On average you should expect to spend about 15 minutes each day.

In what environment should you take the test?

Ideally you take the study in an indoor setting where you can turn off lights and use window blinds to block outdoor lights. Try keeping the lighting condition roughly the same across different days of the longitudinal study.

This is a longitudinal study that will span 9 days. Each day you should expect to spend roughly 15 minutes (with the first three days consuming more time than others).

The goal of this study is to help you name colors (e.g., red, green, etc.) that you usually find confusing. It's not just about telling the difference between two otherwise confusing colors. Rather, we want to help you name common colors so that you can communicate in color vocabulary with color normal individuals.

To that end, this study has three modules: Color Matching, Training, and Test. Depending on which day you are on during the study, you will go through some, or all, of the modules, as described below.

Read the description carefully and click the row that corresponds to your current day of the study.
